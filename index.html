Papers, Essays & Notes
Macroeconomic Data Lab combines reproducible public data with interactive visualisation and original written research. It retrieves annual real GDP growth and consumer-price inflation for Turkey, the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, and Japan from the World Bank API, alongside two Data Lab research notes and eight macroeconomic essays, working papers, and case studies available in the Research & Essays archive.

Why I Built This

I built this project to move beyond headlines and compare the same macroeconomic measures across economies exposed to a shared global disruption. The contrast between Turkey, advanced European economies, the United States, and Japan made it a useful way to ask how similar shocks can be associated with very different inflation and growth paths.

The main lesson was methodological: a transparent model with stated assumptions is more useful than a sophisticated label without evidence. Working with API data also made clear why definitions, missing observations, and the difference between correlation and causation matter in empirical economics.

Working Paper · WP-01 6 Pages JEL: E31, E52, E58, F41

Inflation Persistence and Monetary Policy Effectiveness: Evidence from Emerging Economies

Independent Student Working Paper

Abstract: This paper investigates the relationship between inflation persistence and monetary policy effectiveness in emerging economies. Using panel data from a cross-section of emerging markets, we estimate an autoregressive model of inflation and a fixed-effects panel specification that incorporates monetary policy instruments, exchange rate dynamics, and institutional factors. Our results indicate that inflation persistence is high, with the lagged inflation coefficient estimated at approximately 0.72. While contractionary monetary policy exerts a statistically significant downward pressure on inflation, its effectiveness is constrained by structural vulnerabilities including exchange rate pass-through, fiscal imbalances, and weak institutional frameworks.

The Credibility Gap: How Turkey’s Central Bank Lost and Tried to Reclaim Monetary Authority

Independent Student Research Essay

Summary: When a central bank raises interest rates, the conventional expectation is that inflation will fall—yet this mechanism depends on the belief that the central bank is capable of and committed to following through. Descriptive Readout (2019-2023)

The selected series show a shared pandemic-era downturn followed by different recovery profiles. Turkey's annual growth rebounded strongly in 2021 while consumer-price inflation subsequently rose sharply. Germany's output path weakened in 2023, while the US experienced a comparatively stronger growth outcome over the same year.

These patterns are descriptive, not proof of policy effects. Countries differ in energy exposure, trade links, exchange-rate regimes, fiscal policy, and measurement choices; identifying any of these mechanisms would require a broader dataset and an estimated model.

Reading List & Intellectual Influences
Foundational books, econometric papers, and regulatory texts that shaped my essays—alongside my student reflections on how each source changed the questions I asked.
Institutional Economics & Critical Junctures · Shaped Essay 3 & Essay 4
Daron Acemoğlu & James A. Robinson (2012) — Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty

How it shaped my thinking: Before reading Acemoğlu and Robinson, my instinct was to treat currency depreciation or high inequality as the result of isolated technical policy mistakes. Their distinction between inclusive and extractive institutions—and how "critical junctures" lock in political equilibria—gave me the analytical spine for both my essay on the Turkish lira and my comparative essay on Gulf petrostates versus Norway. It pushed me to ask not merely what interest-rate or fiscal decision a government made, but which institutional constraints allowed that decision to persist.

Hybrid New Keynesian Phillips Curve (NKPC) · Shaped Working Paper WP-01 & Credibility Gap Essay
Jordi Galí & Mark Gertler (1999) — Inflation Dynamics: A Structural Econometric Analysis & Guillermo Calvo (1983) — Staggered Prices

How it shaped my thinking: Galí and Gertler’s hybrid Phillips Curve specification helped me formalize a phenomenon I had witnessed firsthand in Turkey: why inflation keeps rising even after a central bank begins tightening. When institutional credibility erodes, price- and wage-setters abandon forward-looking targets and index their decisions to past inflation realizations. That shift from rational forward-looking expectations to adaptive backward-looking behavior is what produces the high autoregressive persistence coefficient (β ≈ 0.72) examined in WP-01.

Monetary Policy Rules & Emerging Market Constraints · Shaped WP-01 & Essay 1 (ERPT)
John B. Taylor (1993) — Discretion Versus Policy Rules in Practice & Frederic S. Mishkin (2000) — Inflation Targeting in Emerging-Market Countries

How it shaped my thinking: Taylor (1993) demonstrates why nominal interest rates must rise more than one-for-one with inflation (the Taylor Principle) to raise the real interest rate and cool demand. Pairing Taylor’s rule with Mishkin (2000) clarified why monetary transmission is much more fragile in emerging economies: when fiscal dominance, foreign-currency liabilities, and high exchange-rate pass-through (ERPT) are present, interest-rate adjustments alone cannot anchor inflation without institutional and fiscal reform.

Capital Accumulation (r > g) & Rentier State Theory · Shaped Essay 4 & Essay 2
Thomas Piketty (2014) — Capital in the Twenty-First Century & Hazem Beblawi (1987) — The Rentier State in the Arab World

How it shaped my thinking: Piketty’s r > g inequality explains how capital returns compound faster than output growth in market economies, but it did not fully explain why hydrocarbon-rich Gulf monarchies exhibit such rigid political and social stratification compared to Norway. Beblawi’s "rentier bargain" supplied the missing mechanism: when a state finances itself through external resource rents rather than domestic taxation, it severs the historical link between taxation and representative accountability—freezing existing hierarchies in place.

Liquidity Transformation, Fire-Sale Contagion & Microstructure · Shaped Borsa Istanbul Essay
Capital Markets Board of Turkey (SPK) Communiqué III-52.1 & Classic Open-Ended Fund Run Literature

How it shaped my thinking: Analyzing the September 2026 TEFAS investment fund liquidations and VIOP margin calls in Borsa Istanbul taught me how regulatory rules interact with market microstructure. Offering T+1/T+2 daily cash redemptions on funds heavily concentrated in illiquid, low-float stocks creates a textbook liquidity mismatch: when redemptions spike, managers are forced to dump liquid BIST 30 heavyweights at fire-sale prices, transmitting a localized fund run into a market-wide margin spiral.

Resource-Based View (VRIO) & Activity-System Complementarity · Shaped Inditex Case Study
Michael E. Porter (1985, 1996) — Competitive Advantage & What Is Strategy? & Jay Barney (1991) — Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage

How it shaped my thinking: Deconstructing Inditex through Porter’s Five Forces and Barney’s VRIO framework showed me why competitive advantage rarely resides in a single isolated capability like "fast shipping." Individual operational practices can be copied; what is difficult to imitate is a tightly coordinated feedback system linking store-level demand data, design, near-shore sourcing, and inventory allocation—though that same interdependence creates strategic trade-offs against ultra-fast C2M algorithms and sustainability mandates.

Methods & Sources
What this project measures, what it does not establish, and how to reproduce the displayed data.

The data chart uses World Bank indicator NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG for annual real GDP growth and FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG for annual consumer-price inflation. The page requests the 2019-2023 observations directly from the World Bank API for the five selected economies and displays a clearly labelled fallback dataset only when that request cannot be completed.

Important limitation: the simulator applies hand-set elasticities to create a scenario extension. It is not an estimated SVAR, a regression, or a forecast. It is included to make assumptions visible and to show how a future version of the project could be tested with a larger, documented econometric model.
