Why I built this

Living in a country with high inflation made me want to look at the data, not only the headlines. So I compared the same two measures, real GDP growth and inflation, in five economies from 2019 to 2023. Turkey’s inflation was far higher than in the other four.

My work follows one question. First, how much people trust institutions: my essays on Turkey’s central bank and the lira. Then, how firms decide when they cannot trust the system around them: my projects on company annual reports (10-Ks) and investment banks. Then, how a firm keeps control of its own system: my Inditex case study. The numbers show me what happened, but they do not prove why. Working with API data also showed me that definitions and missing data matter, and that correlation is not causation.

Sports Economics Arsenal FC annual reports 10 seasons (2015/16 to 2024/25)

Arsenal FC: Club Finances and Results on the Pitch

Question: To what extent were changes in Arsenal’s revenue, wages and transfer spending associated with changes in league performance?

Data and method: A 10-season dataset from Arsenal’s audited accounts (Companies House), 128 transfers and public match statistics (points, goal difference, expected-goal difference). I compare how the money and the results moved together, including with a one-season lag.

Result and limit: Transfer spending was more closely linked to the next season’s expected points than to the same season’s (r = 0.62, nine season pairs). The limit is the size: nine pairs from one club. I treat it as a pattern to test, not as proof.

Company Valuation SEC Form 10-K filings Python · Streamlit

Do Company Annual Reports Help Predict Future Profit Margins?

Question: Does the written text in U.S. company annual reports (Form 10-K) add useful information for predicting future operating margins, beyond standard accounting numbers?

Data and method: 10-K filings and financial data from SEC EDGAR for 30 US non-financial firms, 2014 to 2024. I test whether text features improve forecasts of changes in operating margin, using only information public at each filing date (no “looking ahead”). The repository also has a simple discounted-cash-flow valuation tool and a dashboard.

Result and limit: Negative. The text added no predictive power over accounting ratios (p = 0.23), and adding all twelve text features made the forecasts worse. The limit is the sample: 30 firms, and no financial firms.

Banking Crises World Bank + IMF crisis dates Python

Can Public Data Give Early Warning of Banking Crises?

Question: Using publicly available economic data, can simple models flag countries at higher risk of a systemic banking crisis one year ahead?

Data and method: World Bank indicators for 76 economies (1990 to 2025) and the IMF’s banking-crisis dates. For each year from 2000 to 2008, I test whether simple indicators flag a crisis one year ahead, training only on earlier years.

Result and limit: Adding private credit and current-account balances raised the crises caught from 4 to 9 of 19. But all nine came in the year before 2008, and outside that year the model caught 0 of 4. So the result is unstable over time. Random forests did not beat logistic regression.

Investment Banking 40 SEC 10-K reports 8 U.S. firms (2020 to 2024)

How Do Investment Banks Use Their Profits?

Question: How did eight U.S. investment-banking firms split their capital between keeping reserves, reinvesting, and paying shareholders between 2020 and 2024?

Data and method: Figures I collected by hand from 40 Form 10-K reports of Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan Chase, Stifel, Evercore, Lazard, Moelis and Jefferies. I compare the large banks with the smaller advisory firms.

Result and limit: Buybacks jumped in the 2021 boom but had almost no link to the banks’ next-year return on equity (r = -0.049). Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan kept CET1 (regulatory capital) ratios of 13.0% to 17.4%. In the 2023 slowdown, the advisory boutiques kept paying their staff while fees fell, so pay rose to as much as 71.6% of revenue and their margins dropped. The limit: eight firms show what they did, not why.

Financial Stability Essay 5 Pages Capital Markets & Microstructure

The Fragility of Liquidity: Fund Runs, Fire Sales and Regulatory Intervention in Borsa Istanbul

Market Microstructure, TEFAS Redemption Mismatches, and Regulatory Trade-offs
Independent Student Research Essay

Summary: In September 2026 the Capital Markets Board (SPK) suspended 131 funds on TEFAS, Turkey's online fund platform, and ordered them into liquidation. I asked why. These funds promised to pay investors back within one or two days but held large stakes in shares that trade very little, so when many investors asked for their money at once the funds had to sell fast at low prices (a fire sale), which pushed prices down further. I propose liquidity buffers, limits on concentrated positions and longer notice periods for redemptions, but rescuing funds has a cost too: if managers expect help, they may take the same risks again.

Macroeconomic Policy Essay 1 Page Monetary Authority & Expectations

The Credibility Gap: How Turkey’s Central Bank Lost and Tried to Reclaim Monetary Authority

Independent Student Research Essay

Summary: I asked why Turkey's inflation stayed so high. A rate rise only works if people believe the central bank will keep going, and Turkey replaced four central bank governors between July 2019 and June 2023. Governor Ağbal was dismissed in March 2021, two days after raising the policy rate to 19%, and after 2023 the bank went back to orthodox policy. I argue that lost trust was one main cause, but the rate cuts that followed also played a part.

Academic Essay · Essay 3 3 Pages Currency & Institutional Economics

The Lira Doesn’t Lie: Institutional Foundations of Currency Depreciation in Turkey

Monetary Credibility, Current Account Imbalances, and the Political Economy of Exchange Rate Decline
Independent Student Research Essay

Summary: The lira lost about 97% of its value against the dollar between 2008 and mid-2025, and I wanted to know why. I argue that the main reason is weak institutions, not only monetary policy: Turkey produces a narrow range of goods, has a large current account deficit (about $41.8 billion in 2023 in revised central bank data, meaning it buys much more from abroad than it sells) and has a central bank that people trust less. Short-term fixes can calm the currency, but I think they will not last without deeper institutional change.

Political Economy Essay · Essay 4 3 Pages Institutional Economics & Inequality

The Paradox of Petrowealth: Resource Rents, Institutional Sequencing, and Inequality in the Gulf

Comparing the Arabian Peninsula and the Norwegian Model Through the Lens of Institutional Economics
Independent Student Research Essay

Summary: I asked why oil made the Gulf monarchies more unequal while Norway, which found oil in 1969, became one of the most equal countries, with a state fund worth about $2.3 trillion by mid-2026. Using Piketty's r > g, Beblawi's idea of the rentier state (a state that lives on oil income instead of taxes) and Acemoğlu and Robinson's critical junctures, I argue that oil makes stronger whatever institutions a country already has when it finds it. This is a comparison of a few countries, so it shows a pattern, not a rule.

Position Essay · Essay 2 2 Pages Corporate Strategy & Governance

Profit First: Why ESG Works Only When It Pays

Reputation, Cost of Capital, Carbon Pricing and the Limits of ESG Ratings
Independent Student Essay

Summary: My view is that for most firms profit comes first, so ESG works when it pays. It pays through reputation, cheaper borrowing for well-governed firms and access to EU markets under the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), a carbon charge on some imports from 1 January 2026. It fails with greenwashing and with ratings that disagree: Berg, Kölbel and Rigobon found an average correlation of only 0.54 between ESG raters, and 0.30 for governance. So, building on Friedman, I think rules such as carbon pricing change company behaviour more than asking firms to be good.

Strategic Case Study 5 Pages Competitive Strategy & Operations

Inditex: Competitive Advantage and Strategic Trade-offs in Global Fashion Retail

Independent Student Case Study

Summary: I asked why rivals find it so hard to copy Zara's owner, Inditex. Using Porter's Five Forces, the value chain and VRIO (value, rarity, how hard it is to copy, organisation), I found that rivals can copy single parts, like near-market production with lead times of two to three weeks, but not the way Inditex links store data, design, production and stock into one system. The same links also make it fragile, because a problem in one part slows the whole system, and cheaper online rivals such as Shein and Temu put pressure on its model.

Reading List & Intellectual Influences
Books, papers and rules that helped me write my essays, with a short note on what I took from each one.
Institutional Economics & Critical Junctures · Shaped Essay 3 & Essay 4
Daron Acemoğlu & James A. Robinson (2012) Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty

How it shaped my thinking: Before reading this book, I thought problems like a falling currency or high inequality came from separate policy mistakes. Acemoğlu and Robinson showed me that institutions matter: some countries have rules that work for most people, and others have rules that work for a small group at the top. After this, I started to ask which rules allowed a bad decision to continue for so long. This idea is behind my essay on the Turkish lira and my essay comparing the Gulf oil states with Norway.

Hybrid New Keynesian Phillips Curve (NKPC) · Shaped The Credibility Gap
Jordi Galí & Mark Gertler (1999) Inflation Dynamics: A Structural Econometric Analysis & Guillermo Calvo (1983) Staggered Prices

How it shaped my thinking: I have not worked through the econometrics in this paper, but I learned its main idea: when people stop trusting the central bank, they set prices and wages by looking at last year's inflation instead of the bank's target. This helped me understand why inflation in Turkey stayed high, and it is one of the ideas behind my essay on the Turkish central bank.

Monetary Policy Rules & Emerging Market Constraints · Shaped The Lira Doesn’t Lie
John B. Taylor (1993) Discretion Versus Policy Rules in Practice & Frederic S. Mishkin (2000) Inflation Targeting in Emerging-Market Countries

How it shaped my thinking: At first I thought a central bank could stop inflation just by raising interest rates. From Taylor (1993) I learned the main idea: when inflation goes up, the interest rate has to go up by more, so that the real rate rises and people spend less. Mishkin (2000) showed me why this works less well in emerging countries like Turkey, where government spending and debts in foreign currency make rate changes weaker. I used these ideas in my essay The Lira Doesn’t Lie.

Capital Accumulation (r > g) & Rentier State Theory · Shaped Essay 4
Thomas Piketty (2014) Capital in the Twenty-First Century & Hazem Beblawi (1987) The Rentier State in the Arab World

How it shaped my thinking: Piketty’s idea r > g, that money earned from wealth grows faster than the economy, helps explain inequality, but it did not explain why the Gulf oil states keep such fixed social and political hierarchies compared with Norway. From Beblawi (1987) I learned the main idea of the rentier state: when a government lives on oil money instead of taxes, people have less power to ask it for anything in return. This idea is at the centre of my essay The Paradox of Petrowealth.

Liquidity Transformation, Fire-Sale Contagion & Microstructure · Shaped Borsa Istanbul Essay
Capital Markets Board of Turkey (SPK) Communiqué III-52.1 & Classic Open-Ended Fund Run Literature

How it shaped my thinking: When I studied the September 2026 fund suspensions, I saw a simple problem: funds promised to pay investors cash within one or two days, but many of them held shares in small companies that are hard to sell quickly. When many investors asked for their money at once in September 2026, managers had to sell the big, easy-to-sell BIST 30 shares at low prices, and the losses spread across the market. This is the main idea of my Borsa Istanbul essay, The Fragility of Liquidity.

Resource-Based View (VRIO) & Activity-System Complementarity · Shaped Inditex Case Study
Michael E. Porter (1985, 1996) Competitive Advantage & What Is Strategy? & Jay Barney (1991) Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage

How it shaped my thinking: At first I thought Zara was successful because of one thing, its fast shipping. Porter’s Five Forces and the main idea of Barney (1991), that a strength only lasts if rivals cannot copy it, showed me that single practices are easy to copy but the way Zara links store sales data, design, nearby production and stock is not. The same links also bring trade-offs, for example against faster online rivals and new sustainability rules. This is what my Inditex case study is built on.

Theory of Constraints & Bottlenecks · Shaped how I read the Inditex case
Eliyahu M. Goldratt & Jeff Cox (1984) The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement

How it shaped my thinking: Before this book, I thought hard work and high efficiency in every department meant success. Goldratt shows that the bottleneck, the slowest step, decides how much the whole system can produce. If every other part tries to be as efficient as possible on its own, the flow breaks, and the wrong measurements make the problem worse. This helped me see why Zara's linked system is strong but also fragile, and why a firm has to improve the whole system first.

Methods & Sources
What the chart shows, what it cannot show, and how to get the same numbers yourself.

The chart uses two World Bank indicators: NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG for annual real GDP growth and FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG for annual consumer-price inflation (CPI). The countries are Turkey, the US, Germany, the UK and Japan, and the years are 2019 to 2023. The page first shows saved values, then asks the World Bank API for the live numbers and replaces them. If the request fails, the saved values stay on screen.

Limits: The Data Lab only shows World Bank figures side by side. It has no interest-rate data and no statistical tests. It is a student project for comparing five economies, and it describes what happened without explaining why.
